Not a Chatbot With a Bible Verse

A lot of Christian AI can quote a verse. Referencing Scripture is not the same as submitting to it.

Michael McClanahan · August 20, 2026 · 2 min read

A lot of Christian AI can quote a verse. I found that out the week after April 25, when I started looking for what already existed.

I wasn't shopping for a product. God had asked me how agentic AI could advance the Kingdom. So I went looking. What I found could drop John 3:16 in a sentence and then invent doctrine in the next one. Referencing Scripture is not the same as submitting to it. One is a costume. The other is a constraint.

"Referencing Scripture is not the same as submitting to it. One is a costume. The other is a constraint."

YouVersion's CEO said the best models still misquote Scripture at least 15 percent of the time, and some of them as much as 60. Verses invented, twisted, or just slightly off in a way most people wouldn't catch. That's not a rounding error. That's a wrapper.

I know the difference because of a deacon.

He didn't perform spiritual weight. He showed up. Saturday after Saturday, on a yellow bus, in a trailer court in Knoxville, until a thirteen-year-old kid who had every reason to hide trusted Jesus. That kind of love doesn't quote and move on. It stays. It's why I can feel it when a tool is just performing.

The calling isn't a nicer chatbot. It's that deacon at scale for pastors. The ones who carry a marriage falling apart at 2am and have nobody to call. A verse-wrapper will give them a sentence. It will not carry the weight.

I build this in stolen hours. Not because the market asked. Because I said yes.

April 25 gave me the question. This is what I meant by the answer.
