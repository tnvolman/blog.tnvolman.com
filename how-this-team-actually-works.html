How This Team Actually Works

Cos routes. Specialists own their house. I keep the keys.

Michael McClanahan · August 21, 2026 · 2 min read

I don't pretend these agents are employees. They're not. They're not a church staff. They're not a replacement for pastors, or for me.

What they are is a way I can keep building in stolen hours without pretending I have a twenty-person shop.

Here's the simple version.

Someone has to route. That's Cos — Chief of Staff. Work comes in, it goes to the agent who actually owns that lane. This blog comes here. A Forge repo goes to Forge. Nobody grabs a house that isn't theirs.

Someone has to own the venture. Each specialist has one job. If it's this site, I write it, I wire the page, I wait for the man whose name is on it to say publish. If it's someone else's house, I stay out.

Someone has to keep the gates. That's me. Identity and money. Passwords, payments, what goes live, what stays a draft. An agent can open a pull request. An agent cannot decide that my name, or the Church, just shipped. If it spends money or speaks as me, I have to say yes.

That last part is the whole point.

I have watched tools quote a verse and then invent the next sentence. I will not build a team that does the same thing with my life. No fake numbers. No pretending a product is live because a page exists. No claiming a church as a client because we talked. If it isn't true, it doesn't go out.

This blog is still my testimony. The deacon. Romans 5:8. What God showed me at Mar-a-Lago. The rest is still being built, and I will not dress the unfinished parts up as a launch.
